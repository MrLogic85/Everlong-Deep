# Pirate Game: Trading Design

Oct 26, 2026 · Fredrik

## Core principle

**Danger is priced in.** Goods that travel short distances, or along safe routes, are less profitable. Profit lives where the risk is, and the risk is mostly pirates.

## Production and needs

Each port has its own production and needs, and these shift over time. What a port makes and what it lacks is part of what makes it different from other ports.

## How prices move

NPC movement and faction trading routes affect prices. Since vessels and their history are already tracked, what they buy and sell can be tracked too, giving a fluctuating economy. This might turn out to be overly complex.

The core principle can emerge rather than being a rule. The same ships that carry rumors carry goods:

- **Safe routes** fill up with traders, who even out prices between ports.
- **Dangerous routes** have few traders, so price gaps stay large.

Nothing has to state that safe routes pay less. It falls out of where NPC traders dare to go.

## Prices are information

Prices are part of the rumor system. You only know what goods cost in a distant port from what someone told you, and that may be stale or wrong. Anyone can deliberately lie to you.

| Source | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Tavern | Cheap and noisy: lots of stories, mostly secondhand, often stale |
| Merchant | Usually better informed, but with their own interests |
| Your own ships and crew | Closest to firsthand: what they saw in port that day |

The accuracy of information varies vastly between sources. Arbitrage becomes a question of whose information is freshest, and a well-placed lie about prices is a weapon.

## Merchant play and trust

Good information comes from people who trust you, so a successful merchant career is about building a network of reliable contacts. That network is what makes trading learnable rather than a gamble.

It's hard to be trusted as a merchant if you're also a pirate. A known pirate gets worse information, because the merchants who know the most are the least willing to share it with someone who might rob them.

## Pirates and merchants: one economy

Pirates and merchants aren't just two roles, they're two halves of one economy. Piracy makes routes dangerous, and danger makes them profitable.

A player who does both can profit from their own reputation. Raid a route enough and goods along it get scarce, prices rise, and trading on that route pays well for anyone willing to risk it, as long as nobody figures out that the pirate and the merchant are the same person.

## Open questions

- How detailed does trade tracking need to be before it becomes overly complex?
- How do production and needs shift over time, and what drives the shifts?
- How does a player build trust with merchant contacts, and how is it lost?
- What goods exist, and do some carry their own risks, such as contraband?
